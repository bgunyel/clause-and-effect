### GH-204.8
- text: Every heading `section` writes down has at least one row recorded
  under it, a `---` subheading's rows counting for the `===` heading above it.
  `section` writes each heading down with the number of rows the ledger held
  when it was printed, and the end-of-run file fails on any heading the next
  one follows, or the ledger ends on, with no row between them.
- from: #204
- kind: defect-permitting
- status: active
- direction: static: it reads the headings the run wrote down against the
  ledger, drives the reading with a fixture, reads back from the ledger that
  the question's own row ran, by its tag and its label, and pins as behaviour
  what a heading printed with `echo` does
- note: A move of checks from the unsplit file into an issue file carries rows
  away from the heading they printed under. A heading moved without its rows,
  or left behind when they moved, prints and asks nothing, and nothing said so.
  Rows printed only inside a subshell are not recorded and do not count, which
  is the #98 self-test's rule for what establishes something. A heading
  printed other than through `section` -- with an `echo` or a `printf`, say --
  has two effects, and neither shows in the output: it is not written down,
  so its rows count for the heading before it; and past a file's opening,
  where `source_checks` has already cleared `REQ`, `REQ` is not cleared, so
  the tag the rows before it ended under carries into its own (review of PR
  #220, rounds 2, 3 and 6). Nothing refuses such a heading; the driver's convention
  says a heading is printed with `section`, and that is all. A row that read
  the suite's text for one was tried and dropped: its label outran its
  pattern twice, `echo; echo "=== x ==="` passing it in round 4 and
  `printf '%s\n\n' '=== x ==='` in round 5, and the only label true by
  construction would have named the pattern and nothing a reader can use. So
  an `echo "=== x ==="` at the start of a line, the spelling a heading copied
  from the `---` subheadings would take, passes green, and that is the trade,
  held by a fixture that prints one heading each way and asks both effects.
  And the question's row is read back from the ledger, because its text pin
  shows that it is written and not that it runs: a `||` ending the line before
  it kept the pin green with the row gone (round 3).

### GH-204.8
- text: Every section heading the suite prints has at least one row recorded
  under it, a `---` subheading's rows counting for the `===` heading above it.
  `section` writes each heading down with the number of rows the ledger held
  when it was printed, and the end-of-run file fails on any heading the next
  one follows, or the ledger ends on, with no row between them. No file of the
  suite prints a `===` heading other than through `section`.
- from: #204
- kind: defect-permitting
- status: active
- direction: static: it reads the headings the run wrote down against the
  ledger, drives the reading with a fixture, reads the suite for a heading
  printed with `echo` or `printf`, and reads back from the ledger that the
  question's own row ran
- note: A move of checks from the unsplit file into an issue file carries rows
  away from the heading they printed under. A heading moved without its rows,
  or left behind when they moved, prints and asks nothing, and nothing said so.
  Rows printed only inside a subshell are not recorded and do not count, which
  is the #98 self-test's rule for what establishes something. A heading
  printed other than through `section` -- with an `echo`, say -- has two
  effects, and neither shows in the output: it is not written down, so its
  rows count for the heading before it; and `REQ` is not cleared, so the tag
  the rows before it ended under carries into its own (review of PR #220,
  rounds 2 and 3). So the suite is read for such a line. What that reading
  does not see, named: a heading printed from a variable, or by a line that
  does not open with `echo` or `printf` and, after any options or a `%s\n`
  format, the `===`. And the question's row is read back
  from the ledger, because its text pin shows that it is written and not that
  it runs: a `||` ending the line before it kept the pin green with the row
  gone (round 3).
